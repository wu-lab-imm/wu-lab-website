bodyEn: >-
  L-Proline is not only an important amino acid for protein synthesis and
  cellular metabolism, but also acts as a neuromodulator in the central nervous
  system. Abnormal proline homeostasis has been associated with cognitive
  impairment and neuropsychiatric disorders, including schizophrenia and autism
  spectrum disorder.


  The high-affinity proline transporter (PROT/SLC6A7) is enriched at presynaptic
  glutamatergic terminals and regulates extracellular L-proline levels through
  Na⁺- and Cl⁻-coupled uptake.


  On May 6, 2026, the Wu Lab at the Institute of Materia Medica, Chinese Academy
  of Medical Sciences and Peking Union Medical College, published a study
  entitled **“Structural basis for the transport mechanism and cholesterol
  modulation of the human proline transporter”** in **Nature Communications**.


  Using single-particle cryo-electron microscopy combined with functional
  assays, the study determined high-resolution structures of human PROT in
  L-proline-bound and substrate-free states, revealing the molecular basis of
  substrate selectivity, ion coupling, and conformational transition.


  Unexpectedly, the study further discovered that membrane cholesterol can
  directly enter the orthosteric substrate-binding pocket of PROT and compete
  with L-proline, uncovering a distinct mechanism of lipid-mediated transporter
  regulation.


  ## Major findings


  ### Molecular basis of L-proline recognition


  L-Proline binds within the central substrate-binding pocket formed by TM1,
  TM3, TM6, and TM8.


  Its carboxylate group is coordinated through hydrogen-bonding interactions and
  the Na1 site, whereas its rigid pyrrolidine ring occupies a compact
  hydrophobic pocket formed by residues including Y53, Y133, Y297, G300, F303,
  and F403.


  Comparison with GlyT1 reveals that F303 and F403 are key determinants of PROT
  function. F303 acts as a steric gatekeeper controlling substrate size, whereas
  F403 optimizes L-proline binding and transport.


  Remarkably, substitution of F303 with the corresponding GlyT1 residue enables
  PROT to transport glycine, directly demonstrating how subtle differences
  within the conserved SLC6 binding pocket determine substrate specificity.


  ### Conformational transition during substrate release


  The L-proline-bound PROT structure adopts an occluded conformation, whereas
  substrate-free PROT adopts an inward-open conformation.


  Transition between these states involves a large rearrangement of TM1a, which
  moves approximately 24 Å and rotates by approximately 51° toward the membrane
  plane.


  This motion opens the intracellular pathway, accompanied by an approximately
  90° rotation of the substrate-binding residue Y53 and disruption of the Na2
  site, thereby coupling intracellular gate opening with sodium and substrate
  release.


  ### Cholesterol directly occupies the orthosteric substrate-binding pocket


  A striking finding emerged from the substrate-free inward-open structures, in
  which a cholesterol molecule was observed extending directly into the central
  substrate-binding pocket. This cholesterol-binding site was designated CHOL0.


  The sterol engages several residues that normally participate in L-proline
  recognition and physically occupies the space required for substrate binding.


  Thus, rather than acting solely through peripheral allosteric sites or changes
  in membrane properties, cholesterol can function as a direct orthosteric
  competitor of L-proline.


  ### Functional validation of cholesterol-mediated inhibition


  Partial depletion of membrane cholesterol using MβCD reduced the apparent Km
  for L-proline uptake from approximately 60.7 μM to 34.0 μM without
  significantly altering Vmax.


  Structure-guided mutations designed to block the CHOL0 pocket similarly
  produced a constitutive low-Km phenotype and abolished the additional effect
  of cholesterol depletion.


  Together, these experiments demonstrate that cholesterol directly modulates
  the apparent substrate affinity of PROT through occupation of the CHOL0 site.


  ### A conformation-dependent cholesterol site


  In addition to CHOL0, several peripheral cholesterol-binding sites were
  identified around PROT.


  Among them, CHOL1′ represents a distinctive conformation-dependent site formed
  between TM1, TM5, and TM7. The pocket is accessible only in the inward-open
  state.


  During transition toward the occluded conformation, coordinated movements of
  TM1a and IL2 collapse the CHOL1′ tunnel, preventing cholesterol binding.


  This observation suggests that cholesterol binding may also be dynamically
  coupled to the conformational cycle of the transporter.


  ## Significance


  This study provides a structural framework for substrate recognition, ion
  coupling, and conformational transitions in human PROT.


  More importantly, it reveals an unexpected mode of membrane-protein regulation
  in which cholesterol itself functions as an endogenous orthosteric ligand that
  directly competes with L-proline for the substrate-binding pocket.


  These findings establish a direct molecular connection between membrane lipid
  homeostasis and neuronal amino-acid transport, expanding the conventional view
  of cholesterol from a passive membrane component or peripheral allosteric
  regulator to an active participant in substrate recognition.


  The work provides new insight into lipid–transporter coupling and a structural
  foundation for developing therapeutic strategies targeting PROT in
  neurological and neuropsychiatric disorders.


  Yue Zhou, Ningqin Wang, and Jiaqi Wang contributed equally to this work, and
  Jing-Xiang Wu is the corresponding author.

L-脯氨酸（L-proline）不仅是蛋白质合成和细胞代谢的重要氨基酸，也能够在中枢神经系统中发挥神经调节作用。脑内脯氨酸水平异常与认知障碍、精神分裂症和自闭症谱系障碍等多种神经精神疾病相关。

高亲和力脯氨酸转运体（proline transporter, PROT/SLC6A7）主要分布于谷氨酸能神经元突触前末梢，通过Na⁺和Cl⁻电化学梯度驱动L-脯氨酸再摄取，从而精细调控突触间隙中的脯氨酸水平以及兴奋性神经传递。

2026年5月6日，中国医学科学院药物研究所天然药物及核药基础与新药创制全国重点实验室吴惊香课题组在 **Nature Communications** 在线发表题为 **“Structural basis for the transport mechanism and cholesterol modulation of the human proline transporter”** 的研究论文。

该研究利用单颗粒冷冻电镜结合细胞功能实验，解析了人源PROT在L-脯氨酸结合和无底物条件下的多个高分辨率结构，揭示了PROT底物选择、离子耦联及构象转换机制。更为重要的是，研究发现膜胆固醇能够直接进入PROT的正构底物结合口袋，与L-脯氨酸竞争，从而调控PROT的转运活性。

## 主要发现

### 1. 揭示PROT选择性识别L-脯氨酸的分子基础

L-脯氨酸结合于由TM1、TM3、TM6和TM8共同围成的中央底物结合口袋。

其羧基通过氢键以及Na1位点与PROT形成稳定相互作用，而具有刚性环状结构的吡咯烷基团则插入由Y53、Y133、Y297、G300、F303和F403等残基形成的紧凑疏水口袋。

通过与同属SLC6家族的甘氨酸转运体GlyT1比较，研究发现F303和F403是塑造PROT底物特异性的关键残基。

其中，F303相当于控制底物尺寸的“空间门控”残基。将其突变为GlyT1对应的色氨酸后，PROT获得了转运甘氨酸的能力；而F403则主要参与稳定L-脯氨酸结合并维持高效转运。

这些结果揭示了高度同源的SLC6氨基酸转运体如何通过少数关键残基实现不同底物选择性。

### 2. 捕获PROT由封闭状态向内向开放状态的构象转换

L-脯氨酸结合的PROT处于封闭构象，而无底物PROT主要处于内向开放构象。

从封闭状态向内向开放状态转换过程中，TM1a发生大幅度构象变化，向膜平面方向移动约24 Å并旋转约51°，从而打开由中央底物结合口袋通向胞质的通路。

与此同时，参与底物识别的Y53发生约90°侧链翻转，进一步扩大胞内释放通路，为L-脯氨酸从中央结合位点向胞质释放提供结构基础。

该构象变化还导致Na2结合位点瓦解，从而将TM1a运动、Na⁺释放以及底物释放连接为连续的转运过程。

### 3. 发现胆固醇直接进入PROT的正构底物结合位点

在无L-脯氨酸的内向开放PROT结构中，研究观察到一个出乎意料的胆固醇分子直接进入中央底物结合区域，并将这一位点命名为CHOL0。

CHOL0的甾醇环深入PROT胞内通路，其疏水尾部进一步延伸至正常情况下容纳L-脯氨酸的中央结合口袋，并与Y133、Y297和F403等多个底物识别残基形成相互作用。

因此，胆固醇并非仅结合于转运体外围，而是能够直接占据L-脯氨酸的正构结合空间，从物理上阻断底物进入。

这一结合模式揭示了一种不同于经典SLC6胆固醇变构调控的机制——**膜胆固醇本身可以作为PROT的正构竞争配体。**

### 4. 功能实验验证胆固醇竞争性调控L-脯氨酸转运

为了验证结构中观察到的CHOL0是否具有功能意义，研究利用MβCD部分降低细胞膜胆固醇水平，并检测PROT介导的L-脯氨酸摄取。

当膜胆固醇降低约25%后，L-脯氨酸转运的Km由约60.7 μM下降至34.0 μM，而Vmax基本不变，表明降低胆固醇水平能够提高PROT对L-脯氨酸的表观亲和力。

进一步针对CHOL0口袋设计的S238F、A402F和F309W突变同样表现出低Km表型，并且在进一步去除胆固醇后不再发生明显变化。

这些结果证明，胆固醇主要通过直接占据CHOL0正构位点竞争性调节L-脯氨酸转运。

### 5. 发现构象依赖的胆固醇结合位点CHOL1′

除CHOL0外，研究还在PROT周围识别出多个胆固醇或脂质结合位点。

其中，一个位于TM1、TM5和TM7之间的胆固醇结合位点表现出明显的构象依赖性，被命名为CHOL1′。

CHOL1′只在内向开放构象中形成。随着PROT从内向开放状态向封闭状态转换，TM1a和胞内环IL2协同移动并占据该疏水通道，使CHOL1′关闭并排斥胆固醇。

这一结果表明，胆固醇与PROT之间的相互作用不仅能够直接竞争底物，还可能与转运体自身的构象循环相耦联。

## 研究意义

该研究建立了人源PROT底物识别、离子耦联以及从封闭到内向开放状态转换的结构框架，并揭示了PROT选择性识别环状L-脯氨酸的分子基础。

更为重要的是，研究发现胆固醇能够作为一种内源性膜脂配体，直接进入PROT的正构底物结合口袋并与L-脯氨酸竞争，从而调节转运体对底物的表观亲和力。

不同于传统上将胆固醇视为改变膜物理性质或结合转运蛋白外围变构位点的调节因子，这一结果表明，膜脂能够直接参与转运蛋白的底物识别过程。

该研究由此建立了膜胆固醇稳态与神经氨基酸转运之间的直接分子联系，为理解脂质环境如何调控神经递质转运，并进一步开发靶向PROT的神经系统疾病干预策略提供了新的结构基础。
